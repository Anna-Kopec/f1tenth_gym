\doxysubsection{Detailed Description}
2D Li\+DAR simulation: ray tracing against an occupancy-\/grid map, adding other vehicles to a scan, and time-\/to-\/collision checks. 

\hypertarget{laser__models_8py_laser_models_map_raytrace}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{How map scans are simulated}{How map scans are simulated}}\label{laser__models_8py_laser_models_map_raytrace}
The map is a black-\/and-\/white image (black = obstacle) plus a YAML file giving its resolution (metres per pixel) and the world pose of its bottom-\/left corner.


\begin{DoxyEnumerate}
\item {\bfseries{Distance transform (once, when the map loads):}} get\+\_\+dt() computes, for every free cell, the distance in metres to the nearest obstacle cell.
\item {\bfseries{Sphere tracing (every beam, every scan):}} trace\+\_\+ray() starts at the sensor and looks up the distance to the nearest obstacle, d. No obstacle is closer than d in {\itshape any} direction, so the ray can safely jump d metres forward. It repeats until d is below a small tolerance {\ttfamily eps} (the ray has hit something) or the total length passes {\ttfamily max\+\_\+range}. Rays in open space take a few big steps; rays close to walls take many small steps.
\end{DoxyEnumerate}

To avoid computing sin/\+cos for every beam, beam directions come from a lookup table that splits the full circle into {\ttfamily theta\+\_\+dis} (2000 by default) discrete angles.\hypertarget{laser__models_8py_laser_models_agent_raycast}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{How other vehicles are added}{How other vehicles are added}}\label{laser__models_8py_laser_models_agent_raycast}
The map scan only contains static walls. ray\+\_\+cast() then models each opponent as a 4-\/sided polygon and intersects every beam that could hit it with its four edges (get\+\_\+range()). Any beam that hits an opponent before its current range is shortened.\hypertarget{laser__models_8py_laser_models_ttc}{}\doxysubsection{\texorpdfstring{Time-\/to-\/collision}{Time-\/to-\/collision}}\label{laser__models_8py_laser_models_ttc}
check\+\_\+ttc\+\_\+jit() decides whether the car has hit a wall by computing an instantaneous time-\/to-\/collision (i\+TTC) along every beam.

Decide whether the vehicle has hit the environment, using instantaneous time-\/to-\/collision (i\+TTC). 

For each beam i: \begin{DoxyVerb}closing speed  = vel * cos(angle_i)                (the car's speed along that beam)
iTTC_i         = (scan_i - side_distance_i) / closing speed
\end{DoxyVerb}


The numerator is the gap between the car body and the obstacle, not the sensor-\/to-\/obstacle distance. A beam with 0 \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<}= i\+TTC \texorpdfstring{$<$}{<} {\ttfamily ttc\+\_\+thresh} means contact is imminent, so the car counts as crashed. Beams pointing away from the direction of travel have a negative closing speed and so a negative i\+TTC, which is ignored. A stationary car (vel == 0) never collides.

{\ttfamily error\+\_\+model=\textquotesingle{}numpy\textquotesingle{}} makes division by zero return inf or nan instead of raising. This happens for beams at exactly +/\+-\/90 degrees, where cos = 0, and those beams are then ignored.

Distance along one beam to a line segment (one edge of another vehicle). 

Finds where the ray o + d1 \texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*} dir meets the segment va + d2 \texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*} (vb -\/ va):
\begin{DoxyItemize}
\item {\ttfamily d1} is the distance along the beam. It must be \texorpdfstring{$>$}{>}= 0, so the hit is in front of the sensor.
\item {\ttfamily d2} is the position along the edge. It must be in \+[0, 1], so the hit is between the two corners.
\end{DoxyItemize}

If the beam is parallel to the edge, it only counts as a hit when the beam lies exactly along the edge\textquotesingle{}s line, in which case the nearer corner is returned.
